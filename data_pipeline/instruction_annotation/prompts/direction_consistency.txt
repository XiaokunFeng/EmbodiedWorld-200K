## Task

You are given two pieces of directional information extracted from a game video trajectory annotation:

1. **`direction_description`** — A concise, hyphen-joined label describing the camera/character's overall movement direction (e.g., `"forward"`, `"right-forward"`, `"left-backward-up"`). Each component is one of: `forward`, `backward`, `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`, `stationary`.

2. **`move_type`** — A free-text annotation describing the character's overall movement direction in natural language (e.g., `"moving forward"`, `"moving toward the front-right"`, `"remaining stationary"`).

Your task is to determine whether these two descriptions refer to **the same set of directional components** on two axes:
- **Forward / Backward axis** (front-back)
- **Left / Right axis**

**Note**: Ignore any up/down (vertical) components in both inputs. Only the horizontal movement directions matter.

---

## Decomposition Rules

### For `direction_description`
Split by `-` to extract directional components. Ignore any `up` / `down` components. Examples:
- `"forward"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **none**
- `"right-forward"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **right**
- `"left-backward-up"` → forward-backward: **backward**, left-right: **left** (ignore `up`)
- `"right-forward-down"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **right** (ignore `down`)
- `"stationary"` → forward-backward: **none**, left-right: **none**

### For `move_type`
Parse the natural language text to identify directional components. Ignore any up/down/vertical keywords. Use the following keyword mapping:
- **forward**: words like *forward*, *ahead*, *front*
- **backward**: words like *backward*, *back*, *rear*, *retreat*, *retreating*
- **left**: the word *left*
- **right**: the word *right*
- **stationary**: words like *stationary*, *remaining still*, *staying*

Examples:
- `"moving toward the front-right"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **right**
- `"moving forward"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **none**
- `"moving toward the lower-right"` → forward-backward: **none**, left-right: **right** (ignore "lower")
- `"remaining stationary"` → forward-backward: **none**, left-right: **none**
- `"moving toward the rear-left"` → forward-backward: **backward**, left-right: **left**
- `"moving toward the front-right while ascending"` → forward-backward: **forward**, left-right: **right** (ignore "ascending")

---

## Consistency Check

Compare the decomposed components of `direction_description` and `move_type` on each axis:
- **forward-backward axis**: both must agree (both forward, both backward, or both none)
- **left-right axis**: both must agree (both left, both right, or both none)

If **both axes match**, the result is `true`. If **any axis differs**, the result is `false`.

### Examples

| direction_description | move_type | FB axis match | LR axis match | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `right-forward` | `moving toward the front-right` | forward=forward | right=right | **true** |
| `right` | `moving toward the front-right` | **none != forward** | right=right | **false** |
| `forward` | `moving forward` | forward=forward | none=none | **true** |
| `left-backward` | `moving toward the rear-left` | backward=backward | left=left | **true** |
| `right-forward` | `moving forward` | forward=forward | **right != none** | **false** |
| `right-forward-up` | `moving toward the upper-right` | **forward != none** | right=right | **false** |
| `left-forward-down` | `moving toward the front-left while descending` | forward=forward | left=left | **true** |
| `stationary` | `remaining stationary` | none=none | none=none | **true** |
